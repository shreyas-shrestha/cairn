Name and logo options

Twelve candidates from three designers, each working one angle. Every mark is shown large and at the 20px size it would have in the app header. Name clashes are from the designers’ memory only: no trademark, domain or app-store checks have been done.

Campus heritage

BurdellDesigner’s pick

George P. Burdell is the alum who has been in every class, club and company, so "ask Burdell" is the natural way to say "ask the whole alumni body".

The mark
A head-and-shoulders figure whose head is a solid ring and whose shoulders are a broken arc: the person who is on every roster but never quite there.
Known name clashes
The Burdell name is already used on campus (a campus store is called Burdell's, and the name appears on many student-run things). Whether Georgia Tech holds a registration on "George P. Burdell" is not checked. No large outside tech product by this name that I know of; not checked.
Whistle

The steam whistle is how Tech calls everyone at once; the app lets one student send a call that the right alumni hear.

The mark
An upright steam whistle on its pipe with three blasts of steam fanning out from the top, a single call reaching out in several directions.
Known name clashes
Known clashes: Whistle (pet GPS trackers, owned by Mars Petcare) and Whistle (sports media company). The plain word is crowded as a product name and a clean domain is unlikely. Trademark status not checked.
Northave

North Avenue is the street every Tech graduate started from (the "North Avenue Trade School"), so it names the shared starting point of every path the app draws.

The mark
A street-grid route running from a filled dot (you, on North Avenue) around a block corner to an open ring (the alum you are trying to reach).
Known name clashes
"North Ave" is used on campus for the North Avenue apartments and dining hall, and "North Avenue" is a common street and business name in many cities. No specific software product known to me; not checked.
Ratcap

The RAT cap is the gold freshman cap you fill in with what you learn at Tech; the app is where a new student starts filling in who they know.

The mark
A side-on freshman cap: a round crown with a button on top sitting above a separate upturned brim band, the place a new student writes things down.
Known name clashes
No product by this name known to me; not checked. Georgia Tech and its alumni association sell and promote RAT caps, so the term is closely tied to official tradition even though the name itself claims no endorsement. "Rat" reads badly to anyone outside Tech.

Connection and geography

MutualDesigner’s pick

Names the exact thing the product finds: the person, club, lab or employer you and an alum have in common, which is what makes the introduction warm.

The mark
Two overlapping circle outlines, you and the alum, with the shared lens between them filled solid to stand for the person or group you both know.
Known name clashes
Mutual is an existing dating app (LDS-focused), and 'mutual' is a generic word in insurance and finance company names (e.g. Liberty Mutual, Mutual of Omaha). 'Mutuals' is also common social-media slang. Domains and trademarks not checked.
Waypoint

A waypoint is the stop in the middle of a route; here it is the labmate or clubmate you pass through to reach the person you actually want to meet.

The mark
A route arc running from one dot to another, with a solid diamond marker sitting on the top of the arc as the middle person the route passes through.
Known name clashes
Heavily used: HashiCorp Waypoint (developer tool), Waypoint (Vice's gaming outlet), and various navigation, travel and consulting firms. Trademarks and domains not checked.
Greatcircle

A great circle is the shortest route between two points on a globe, which matches the product's globe view and its promise of the shortest path to an alum anywhere.

The mark
A globe outline crossed by a single tilted great-circle arc, with two solid dots on the arc marking the two people at either end of the route.
Known name clashes
Great Circle is a well-known 2021 novel by Maggie Shipstead, and Great Circle Mapper is a flight-route mapping site; 'Indiana Jones and the Great Circle' is a 2024 video game. Other software or company uses not checked; trademarks and domains not checked.
Vouch

A warm introduction is someone vouching for you; the word is short, sayable and says why a note sent through a shared connection gets answered.

The mark
A check mark that starts at a solid dot (you), turns at its corner (the person vouching) and ends at an open ring (the alum you are being introduced to).
Known name clashes
Several existing companies: Vouch (business insurance for startups), Vouch (video testimonial / recruiting tool) and Vouch-branded digital identity products. Crowded name; trademarks and domains not checked.

Short, ownable words

CairnDesigner’s pick

A cairn is a stack of stones left by earlier walkers to mark the route (English, from Scottish Gaelic 'carn', a heap of stones); alumni are the people who went ahead and left markers you can follow.

The mark
Three hand-stacked stones, slightly off-centre from each other, forming a trail marker.
Known name clashes
Known: 'Cairn' is a climbing video game by The Game Bakers, and Cairn Energy was a UK oil company (since renamed Capricorn Energy). No alumni or networking product by this name that I know of, but not checked; trademarks not verified.
Wend

To wend is to make your way somewhere, usually by an indirect route (Old English 'wendan', to turn or go; the source of 'went'); it names the path from you to a person through the clubs, labs and employers in between.

The mark
A winding route from a solid dot (you) to an open ring (the alum you are trying to reach).
Known name clashes
Not checked. I know of no prominent product called Wend. 'Wends' is also a historical name for Slavic peoples in Germany, which is unrelated but worth knowing. Trademarks not verified.
Waggle

The waggle dance is how a honeybee tells its hive-mates the direction and distance of something worth finding; here you ask, and the app points you to the right person on the globe. (Honeybees do this, not yellow jackets, so the link to Tech's mascot is by association only.)

The mark
A globe whose tilted axis is a zigzag waggle run, so the circle reads both as the world and as the two return loops of the bee's dance.
Known name clashes
Crowded. I am aware of Waggle, an adaptive-learning product for schools from HMH; a Waggle AI assistant for managers; and Waggle-named golf apparel and pet-monitoring products. Details from memory, not checked; trademarks not verified.
Thrum

A thrum is a low steady hum, like a hive at work or a plucked string (English, imitative); in weaving, thrums are also the loose thread ends left on a loom, which suits a tool that ties loose ends between people.

The mark
A string stretched between two people and drawn mid-vibration: the resting line plus its upper and lower swing.
Known name clashes
Not checked. I do not know of a prominent software product called Thrum, but it is a real word and small brands likely use it. Trademarks not verified.
